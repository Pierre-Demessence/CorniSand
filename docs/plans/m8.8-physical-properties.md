# M8.8 Physical properties

Status: draft, waiting for the user's approval. Runs before M8.1 (`docs/plans/m8.1-materials.md`), which builds its materials on these properties.

## Goal

Materials behave by their real physical properties instead of hand-tuned ranks: heat spreads at its real speed (copper about eight times faster than steel), melting and boiling take their real energy, hot water rises and cold water sinks so a heated pot stirs itself and a pond freezes from the top, gases rise or sink by their weight against the air, lava creeps while water runs, and each powder piles at its own angle. The Powder Toy stays the inspiration; where it simplifies, real physics wins, as long as the frame budget holds.

## Scope

In: a physical scale for the grid; real conductivity, heat capacity and latent heats for every existing row; the air's real heat capacity and a cell–air exchange calibrated against still-air cooling; density by temperature (liquids and gases) and the convection it drives; gas buoyancy against the air; viscosity; angle of repose; fracture toughness driving the crush pressure; existing tests re-measured; the M8 slices written into the roadmap; a `?scene=physics` showcase.

Not in M8.8: new elements (M8.1 onward; tests use test rows), the air's own speeds (sound and wind stay M6's slowed compromise, backlog line), viscosity changing with temperature (backlog line), combustion properties (M8.2), electrical and optical properties (M7), solubility (M8.4). Limits that stay: a cell is one unit of matter in every state (boiling does not expand 1,700×; M6's burst stands in), solids never move (ice cannot float), nothing moves faster than about one cell per dispatch inside the grid, no radiant heat before the lighting work.

## Choices approved by the user

1. Real physics over The Powder Toy's simplifications, within the frame budget.
2. Heat at its real speed on the grid's physical scale (no speed-up factor): a 7 cm steel bar evens out in about 6 minutes at 1x, copper in under a minute, stone in about an hour.
3. This slice runs first and re-derives the existing elements; the M5 and M6 behaviors it changes are re-judged at its gate.
4. Each property enters the table with the mechanic that uses it and a test that shows it reaching the simulation, never as unused data.

## Terms

- **Physical scale**: one cell is `CELL_SIZE` = 1.36 mm and one dispatch `DISPATCH_TIME` = 2.08 ms, the only pair for which `DEFAULT_GRAVITY` (1/32 cell per dispatch²) is 9.81 m/s² at 1x (4 supersteps per frame at 60 fps, 480 dispatches per second). A pair of neighbors exchanges heat once per superstep, `PAIR_TIME` = 2 dispatches.
- **Share**: the fraction of the way to their capacity-weighted mean two cells move in one exchange (`exchangeHeat`), at most 1.
- Units in the table: conductivity W/m·K, heat capacity MJ/m³·K and latent heat MJ/m³ (per cell, one cell being one unit of matter: a gas row's capacity is its specific heat times its liquid's density, as STEAM's 2.0 already is), density kg/m³, viscosity Pa·s, angle of repose degrees, fracture toughness MPa·√m.

## Acceptance criteria

New test titles start with `M8.8 AC<n>:`. Every existing test whose numbers come from the old values is re-measured, not weakened: its intent stays, and each changed bar is logged under its item (old value, new value, why).

Scale and conduction (M8.8c)

- AC1 `src/physics.ts` SHALL derive `CELL_SIZE` and `DISPATCH_TIME` from `DEFAULT_GRAVITY` and 480 dispatches per second (unit test: 1.36 mm and 2.08 ms within 1%).
- AC2 THE SYSTEM SHALL exchange heat between two cells at the share min(1, `PAIR_TIME` / `CELL_SIZE`² × the harmonic mean of their conductivities × (1/Ca + 1/Cb)), in the TypeScript twin and the WGSL (unit test of the twin; GPU single pairs for steel, stone, water and a 2,000 W/m·K test row, which is capped and never passes the mean).
- AC3 WHEN a sealed bar half at 100 °C and half at 0 °C relaxes, THE SYSTEM SHALL decay the difference between its halves with the time constant L² / (π² α) of its material (α = conductivity / capacity) within 15%, for steel, stone and ice (GPU).
- AC4 Every existing row SHALL take its conductivity and heat capacity from physical data, its source cited in the row's comment (WATER's conductivity is water's own 0.6, its convection coming from AC10); heat AC6–AC8 pass, re-measured.
- AC5 Every transition SHALL take its latent heat from physical data per cell (latent heat per kilogram times the denser phase's density, for example water 334 to freeze and 2,257 to boil); heat AC9–AC14 pass, re-measured. WHEN a cell's latent range reaches past `ABSOLUTE_ZERO` or `MAX_TEMP` (steam condensing runs about 1,130 °C under 100 °C), validation SHALL accept it, the stored temperature SHALL keep it without clamping, and `cellTemp`, the readout and the views SHALL still show a temperature inside `ABSOLUTE_ZERO`..`MAX_TEMP` (unit and GPU tests).
- AC6 Every solid with a crush SHALL give its fracture toughness, and its crush pressure SHALL be toughness × `PRESSURE_PER_TOUGHNESS`, one constant anchored so STONE's crush pressure stays M6's 0.45; validation rejects a hand-given crush pressure (unit test; `tests/air-crush.test.ts` unchanged).

The air's heat (M8.8d)

- AC7 The air's heat capacity SHALL be air's physical 1.2 kJ/m³·K per cell, and WHEN a steel block 50 cells wide at 500 °C rests in still air, THE SYSTEM SHALL cool it with an effective surface coefficient within a factor of 2 of still-air natural convection (10 W/m²·K), measured from its cooling time (GPU, `tests/air-heat.test.ts`).
- AC8 The M6 heat tests (the heater's plume, lava pours, AC28 and AC29) SHALL pass, re-measured.

Density by temperature and convection (M8.8e)

- AC9 Liquid rows SHALL give their density at up to four temperatures from data (linear between, flat outside; water's densest at 4 °C); gas rows and the air SHALL follow the ideal-gas law from one density at a reference temperature; powders and solids keep one density (unit test of `densityAt`, the TypeScript twin).
- AC10 WHEN a tank of water at 20 °C stands on a plate held at 90 °C, THE SYSTEM SHALL bring its top layer to half the plate's rise at least 10× sooner than the same tank with flat density, and its hot cells SHALL rise at a speed within a factor of 3 of the free-convection estimate √(g β ΔT L) (GPU, measured).
- AC11 WHEN a tank of water at 10 °C lies under a ceiling held at −20 °C, THE SYSTEM SHALL form its first ice in the top quarter and keep the bottom water at or above 3.5 °C until the top row has frozen over (GPU).
- AC12 WHEN a body of one liquid is at one temperature, THE SYSTEM SHALL move no cell of it by density (still water stays still apart from spreading); determinism and block locality pass.

Gas buoyancy (M8.8f)

- AC13 THE SYSTEM SHALL move a gas cell up or down by its density against the air's density at the air cell's temperature (`ROOM_TEMP` without air), faster the larger the difference: a test gas of 2 kg/m³ at 20 °C settles to the floor of a box; steam at 110 °C rises; smoke from a fire rises and, once within 5 °C of the air, its centroid rises less than a measured distance per 100 supersteps (GPU). SMOKE's row becomes air-like combustion gas (I16 stays open for what burns).

Viscosity (M8.8g)

- AC14 Liquid rows SHALL give their viscosity from data, and a liquid SHALL spread sideways at the speed of a viscous layer `REF_DEPTH` cells deep, capped at one cell per dispatch: water stays at the cap (the dam-break and U-tube tests pass unchanged), and lava's dam-break front SHALL advance within a factor of 3 of that speed, oil's between lava's and water's (GPU, measured).

Angle of repose (M8.8h)

- AC15 Powder rows SHALL give their angle of repose from data, and a pile poured from a point SHALL stand at it within ±4°: sand at its data angle (about 34°) and a test row at 45° (GPU, `tests/metrics.ts` slope); the existing pile tests pass, re-measured.

Budget (M8.8j)

- AC16 Every bench scene's 1x time SHALL stay within 10% or 0.1 ms, whichever is larger, of the M8.8b baseline, and a new bench scene `pot` (a screen-wide tank heated from below, convecting) SHALL stay within the element engine's 4 ms at 1x.

Showcase (M8.8i)

- AC17 `?scene=physics` SHALL load, side by side: a pot of water on a hot plate, dam breaks of water, oil and lava, a sand pile and a gunpowder pile poured from points, a fire under a ceiling, and a pond under a freezing ceiling (`tests/scenes.test.ts`: every cell inside the grid).

## Design

- `src/physics.ts`: `CELL_SIZE`, `DISPATCH_TIME`, `PAIR_TIME` derived from `DEFAULT_GRAVITY` and the 1x rate; `G` = 9.81. Every physical property converts to cells and dispatches here, so a change of scale is one place.
- Conduction: `exchangeHeat` keeps its form (each cell moves toward the capacity-weighted mean, never past it, heat conserved); only the share changes, computed from the pair's physical values. The share for a pair of rows can be precomputed per row (k/C terms) so the shader adds no division per pair beyond today's. Flows smaller than f32 resolution at high temperatures are lost; AC4's sealed-box test measures the drift.
- Latent overshoot past the physical range: the stored temperature is a bookkeeping value while a cell takes in latent heat, and only `cellTemp` is a temperature. The Heat and Cool tools change the stored value and clamp `cellTemp`'s result, never cutting the latent progress.
- Toughness: fracture toughness from data, how easily a crack runs through the material. It, not the tensile strength of flawless lab samples (where glass beats granite), decides what a blast breaks: every cell is the same size and carries flaws of about the same size, so the pressure that breaks it scales with its toughness. Glass about 0.75, granite about 1.7, ice about 0.1, alumina ceramic about 4; the solids crush in that order (M8.1's crushables follow it), STONE anchoring the scale. Metals, tens of times tougher, never crush.
- Air: `AIR.heatCapacity` becomes air's physical value; the cell–air exchange (`AIR.conductivity`, the coupling pass) becomes a surface coefficient converted by `src/physics.ts`, tuned against AC7's target. The air's buoyancy and speeds stay M6's.
- Density: `densityAt(row, temp)` in TypeScript and WGSL; `cellDensity(c)` uses the cell's `cellTemp` for liquids and gases (the CA already holds every field of an awake block). The air's density at its air cell's temperature comes from `airAround`, which the CA already reads for blocks holding a gas.
- Convection (prototype first, in M8.8e): two vertically adjacent cells of the same liquid swap with a chance that grows with their density difference, scaled so the rise speed meets AC10; a prototype measures the scaling and the cost before the item settles it. A sideways swap is not needed: rising cells carry heat, spreading carries them sideways.
- Gas buoyancy: phase 0's rise becomes a chance from the density difference with the air (or the gas or liquid above), and a heavier gas sinks into empty space or a lighter gas below by the same rule.
- Viscosity: `spreadRow` spreads a liquid with a chance from its viscous spreading speed (ρ g h² / 3μ for a layer `REF_DEPTH` cells deep, in cells per dispatch, capped at 1). `REF_DEPTH` is one constant, set so lava meets AC14.
- Angle of repose (prototype first, in M8.8h): a powder at rest does not slide (static friction); a moving one slides diagonally or sideways with chances that set the pile's angle. The prototype measures the angle against the chances and fits the mapping.
- Validation: each new property has a range check naming the row (`tests/elements.test.ts`), and states that do not use one reject it (a viscosity on a powder).

- **M8** Element catalogue: The Powder Toy's elements that need only what the game simulates before M7 (heat, fire, air and pressure), slice by slice, with real physical properties.   - **M8.8** Physical properties: real heat speed, latent heats, density by temperature and convection, gas buoyancy, viscosity, angle of repose, fracture toughness (this plan). Runs first.
  - **M8.1** Materials and the element picker (`docs/plans/m8.1-materials.md`).
  - **M8.2** Fire and explosives: slow burning (wood, coal, fuse, ignition cord), dust that burns in a flash, flammable gas, plasma, cold flame, pressure-set explosives (C-4, nitroglycerin, diesel), TNT that goes off as a whole (backlog I18), bombs, thermite, fireworks, a cold-set explosive, and stored energy (uranium heats under pressure, vibranium, the singularity); ignition temperature, heat of combustion and burn speed as properties, oxygen in the air.
  - **M8.3** Air, gases and force: heavy gases (carbon dioxide, dry ice), gases that expand when heated or cool when compressed (Boyle gas, refrigerant), vents and vacuum, accelerators and decelerators, repellers, anti-air dust, glow that lights up under pressure, goo, concrete, dust colored by its speed, a wall that opens under pressure.
  - **M8.4** Chemistry: reactions between elements in contact: salt dissolves into salt water up to its solubility, clay and water make paste that bakes into brick, acid dissolves and base neutralizes it, hydrogen burns with oxygen into water (oxygen and liquid oxygen), lithium and rubidium explode in water, iron rusts into broken metal, platinum speeds reactions, soda water fizzes, virus spreads and soap cures it, antimatter annihilates, sponge and gel soak up water, yeast grows, freeze powder.
  - **M8.5** Life: Life-like and Generations rules (Conway's Life and its variants, TPT's set of about 24, rules from LifeWiki's public catalogue) on painted cells, in a pass of their own outside the Margolus CA.
  - **M8.6** Special and growing: clone, breakable clone, converter, void, warp, TRON, plants that drink water and grow, vines, seeds that grow trees, bizarre (it melts when cold).
  - **M8.7** Portals and pipes: portals that carry what enters one to another of the same channel, pipes that carry particles along their length.
