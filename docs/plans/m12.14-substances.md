# M12.14 Substances

Status: M12.14a committed except its bench compare (pending, see the item); M12.14b committed; next M12.14c.

## Goal

Each material is defined once, with all its states and broken forms (water, ice, steam and snow are one material), and keeps what it is made of through every change: each material that melts has its own liquid with its own data, crushed brick stays brick, and cells no longer carry a memory of what they melted from. The GPU still reads one row per state.

## Choices approved by the user

1. Materials are defined as substances with their states inside; the GPU keeps one row per state (the review of the element model: a state worked out from the temperature on every read is ambiguous at a threshold and needs the `temp` word in passes that bind `kind` only).
2. M12.5 (a conserved amount of matter per cell) follows this plan; it is no longer to be confirmed.
3. Metals and rocks boiling is its own slice, M12.15, after M12.5; boiling points that follow the pressure stay in M8.3; melting points stay fixed whatever the pressure.

## Acceptance criteria

- AC1: WHEN a meltable solid or powder of the default table melts, it becomes its own material's liquid row (sand and broken glass: molten glass; gravel: lava), and WHEN that liquid sets, it becomes its material's solid (sand's melt: glass), at the thresholds and latent heats the table has today. Check: `tests/molten.test.ts`, one case per row.
- AC2: WHEN any scene runs, every cell's `kind` bits 16–31 stay 0. Check: the molten test that melts and sets every meltable row reads its `kind` words.
- AC3: Through M12.14a no test's threshold, target or bar changes: tests that set or assert a ctype name the material's own liquid row instead, and every other test passes unchanged (reviewed on the diff).
- AC4: WHEN brick is crushed it becomes brick rubble, which never melts; WHEN ceramic cracks it becomes ceramic shards, which melt at 2,072 °C into molten ceramic and set into ceramic. Check: `tests/air-crush.test.ts`, `tests/shock.test.ts`, `tests/molten.test.ts`.
- AC5: The default table is written as substances, and the flat rows built from it pack byte for byte into the table M12.14b left. Check: a one-off script comparing `packElements` before and after, its result recorded under M12.14c.
- AC6: Each melting point, boiling point and latent heat is written once per substance. WHEN a substance is malformed (a melt or boil without a state on each side, a crush or crack target outside the substance, a melt `into` another liquid at another latent heat or below that liquid's setting point, a liquid with no single solid to set into), the build throws an error naming the substance's first row. Check: `tests/elements.test.ts`.
- AC7: WHEN a row is painted at a temperature, the brush writes the state of its substance at that temperature: water at −10 °C ice, snow at 10 °C water, sand at 1,800 °C molten glass, gravel at 1,300 °C lava, brick rubble at 3,000 °C brick rubble, oil at 300 °C oil flagged as igniting. Check: `tests/picker.test.ts`.

- AC9: Each new liquid's density at its melting point and its change with temperature, conductivity, heat capacity and viscosity come from a source cited in its row comment, or are marked as estimates with a backlog line (as D41). WHEN a sealed one-row bar of each molten metal relaxes from a step, its time constant matches its own data within 15%, as `tests/heat.test.ts` checks solid bars (a bar whose pair share is over 0.15 waits on D40, as `metalBars` does). WHEN columns of lava, molten glass and molten ceramic are released side by side, their fronts order as their viscosities. Check: `tests/molten.test.ts`, `tests/viscosity.test.ts`.
- AC10: The CA time per 1x frame of M12.14a, measured by `npm run bench:compare` from the commit before it, is within the gap between two runs of the same code, recorded under M12.14a.
- AC11: `npm run lint`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm test` and `npm run build` pass after each item.

## Design

**Format.** `SUBSTANCES` in `src/elements.ts` is the hand-written table:

```ts
interface SubstanceDef {
  melt?: { temp: number; latent: number; into?: string };  // every solid and powder state <-> the liquid; `into`: another substance's liquid row
  boil?: { temp: number; latent: number; burst?: number }; // the liquid <-> the gas
  states: readonly StateDef[]; // today's rows without below, molten, setsInto, meltLabel
}
```

- `buildElements(substances)` makes the flat `ElementDef[]` that the GPU, `Simulation` and the tests take today. `ELEMENTS` and `ELEMENT` keep their names, keys, IDs and literal typing. Each solid and powder state's `above` is the melt into the liquid (or `into`); the liquid's `below` sets into the substance's one `solid` state and its `above` is the boil; the gas's `below` is the boil.
- A `StateDef` keeps an `above` only for a change into another substance (oil and gunpowder igniting into fire). `pressureAbove` and `cracksInto` stay inside the substance.
- `validateElements` keeps checking flat rows (IDs, ranges, pairs), so a test table of hand-made flat rows stays valid; `buildElements` adds the substance checks (AC6).
- A substance has no key: errors and `substanceOf(id)` (CPU only) name it by its first row. No substance column reaches the GPU until M12.5 needs one.

**Melts.** One liquid row per material that melts, IDs from 36: MOLTEN_ALUMINIUM, MOLTEN_COPPER, MOLTEN_GOLD, MOLTEN_TITANIUM, MOLTEN_TUNGSTEN, MOLTEN_GLASS (glass, broken glass, and sand by `into`), MOLTEN_CERAMIC, MOLTEN_AEROGEL. LAVA stays basalt's and gravel's, MOLTEN_METAL steel's (label "Molten steel"). Aerogel keeps a liquid of its own that sets back into aerogel: setting into glass would give out glass's latent heat (362 against 21.75 MJ/m³), which only M12.5's amount of matter can fix.

In M12.14a each new row copies its shared row's values, so nothing a test measures changes:
- its density knots are the shared row's scaled by the solid's density over the shared row's (what `cellDensity` did);
- its color is the tint `graphics_molten` drew (`mix(row, solid, 0.6) × 0.65`);
- its `temperature` is the shared row's where that lies above its melting point, else its melting point plus 100 °C.

M12.14d then gives each its own data.

**Rubble.** BRICK_RUBBLE (crushed brick, never melts) and CERAMIC_SHARDS (cracked ceramic, melts with ceramic): powders with their parent's heat capacity and estimated bulk density, conductivity and repose, as gravel's are, each with a Powders button like the other broken forms. GRAVEL becomes basalt's alone.

**Removed with the molten memory.** `molten`, `setsInto`, `meltLabel`, `meltRow`; the ctype in `kind` (bits 16–31 reserved again); `kindMelt` and the `kind*` helpers (a cell's temperature clamp and density read its own row); `withCtype`, `cellCtype`, `elAboveCtype` and `transitions` bits 24–31; `graphics_molten`; the brush uniform's ctype word and the probe's ctype; the ctype argument of `kindWord` and `Canvas.fill`; `effectiveTemp`'s melt row. `cellName` becomes the row's label. The `molten` table word becomes padding.

**Picker.** `paintTarget` picks the state of the row's substance at the paint temperature: above the boil the gas; above the melt the liquid (or `into`); below the melt the row itself if it is a solid or powder, else the substance's solid. A row's own `above` into fire flags ignition as today.

**Risks.** M12.14a touches every shader that reads a cell's thresholds or density; AC3 and `tests/block-locality.test.ts` catch a slip. The bench may move either way: the molten reads are one of D43's suspects.
