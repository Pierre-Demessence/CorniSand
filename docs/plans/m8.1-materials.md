# M8.1 Materials and the element picker

Status: approved; runs after M8.8 (done), whose properties every row here uses, and after M8.9 (done: the scenarios); next M8.1b.

## Goal

Twenty-two new everyday materials (metals from aluminium to tungsten, glass, brick, gravel, snow, sawdust, wax, liquid nitrogen, mercury…) that behave by the heat, phase-change, density and pressure rules the game already has, so those rules are tested on many more cases. Sand melts into glass, hot glass splashed with cold water shatters, and every molten material cools back into what it was. A picker sorted into categories, with search, replaces the single scrolling button bar.

## Scope

In: the picker; `ctype` in `kind` bits 16–23; molten elements that remember what they set into (backlog I15); STONE and BRICK crushed into GRAVEL (STONE's stand-in SAND retires from that role); thermal shock (a solid cracks into its broken form when a neighbor is too much hotter or colder than itself, from real data); the 22 elements below, each with a scene test of its main behavior; a `?scene=materials` showcase; the budget.

Not in M8.1: anything that burns or explodes beyond what exists (M8.2, which also brings ACRYLIC: it burns, and with ALUMINIUM it makes the `iceOnMetal` scenario), reactions between elements (M8.4), heavy gases (M8.3), electricity and light (M7: quartz, distilled water, glass refracting light, insulation blocking current), mercury's swelling with heat (backlog line added by M8.8a).

## Choices approved by the user

1. M8 comes before M7, in the slices listed in `docs/roadmap.md`; the elements that need sparks or energy particles move to M7, those that need Newtonian gravity to M9.
2. The picker as in "Picker" below.
3. A blast crushes stone and brick into gravel, no longer into sand (changes M6 choice 3), at the pressures M8.8's fracture toughness gives (glass breaks before stone).
4. METAL's label becomes "Steel" (it is the steel-like metal among six).
5. Stickmen and fighters (TPT's player characters) are a backlog idea (added by M8.8a), decided after M8: they are a game mechanic of their own, not a material.
6. The UI stays a prototype (no Claude Design canvas); the whole UI is redesigned once every feature exists.
7. Real physics over The Powder Toy's simplifications: every row takes M8.8's physical properties from data.
8. Solids crack from a sudden change of temperature (thermal shock), from real data: quenched hot glass shatters, ice cracks in warm water, and a cold ceramic cup can crack under molten steel unless preheated, as a real one does.
9. The real-world criteria are scenarios (M8.9, `AGENTS.md` "Real-world scenarios"): each item writes its scenarios' tests, with targets from physical data, before its rows and mechanics. ALUMINIUM joins the metals.

## Terms

- **ctype**: a second element ID in a cell, `kind` bits 16–23 (TPT's name). 0 means none.
- **Molten element**: a row with `molten: true` (LAVA, MOLTEN_METAL). Its ctype is the element it sets into; with ctype 0 it sets into its own `below` (painted lava sets into stone, as now).
- **Crush**: a `pressureAbove` transition (M6.5).

## Elements

Each row takes every property M8.8 defines (thresholds, latent heats, density by temperature, conductivity, heat capacity, viscosity, angle of repose, fracture toughness) from physical data, cited in its comment (AGENTS.md "Adding an element"). Crush pressures follow the toughness; ceramic and diamond are never crushed.

| Key | State, category | Main behavior (the scene test) |

| GRAVEL | powder, Powders | What a blast leaves of stone and brick; melts into lava that sets into stone |
| SNOW | powder, Powders | What a blast leaves of ice; melts into water at 0 °C after its latent heat |
| SAWDUST | powder, Powders | Floats on water; flammable |
| BROKEN_GLASS | powder, Powders | What a blast leaves of glass; melts into molten glass |

## Design

### Table

- `ElementDef` gains `category?: Category` and `description?: string` (both required with `toolbar`, which becomes the position inside the category), `molten?: true`, and `Transition.setsInto?: string` (an `above` into a molten element only; default the row itself). `CATEGORIES` in `src/toolbar.ts`: Powders, Liquids, Gases, Solids, Metals, Explosives (FIRE, GUNPOWDER), Special, Life, then Tools (Heat, Cool, Wind). Descriptions are one sentence in our own words, never TPT's.
- Every new row gives the properties M8.8 defines for its state, and the scene tests above run on them.
- Validation: a molten element has a `below`; a `setsInto` element has an `above` into the same molten element (that `above` is the pair the setting uses, so threshold and latent match by construction); the target-range check of a transition into a molten element uses the `setsInto` element's threshold.
- GPU row: `transitions` bits 24–31 hold the `setsInto` ID; `pad1` holds the molten flag. No new row word. The row has 36 words (144 bytes) after M8.8, with the padding words `pad1` to `pad3` free: a new row word takes one of them, and a fourth grows the row to 160 bytes (40 words), which every row of the table pays for.

### ctype and the molten elements

- `cellCtype(c)` and `withCtype(c, k)` in `cellTypesWGSL`; `withType` and `retype` clear bits 16–23, the other helpers keep them; `makeCell` starts at ctype 0. A TS helper `kindWord(type, variant, ctype)` in `src/cell-fields.ts` for scenes and tests. Flyers carry the whole `kind` word, so nothing changes there.
- `cellTemp` and `effectiveTemp`: for a molten cell with ctype k ≠ 0, the lower threshold is `elAboveTemp(k)` and the latent width `elAboveWidth(k) * elHeatCapacity(k) / elHeatCapacity(t)`. Phase 8 compares the same values; a change into a molten element writes the `setsInto` ID. Conductivity and heat capacity stay the molten row's (lava's M6 tuning stays).
- `cellDensity`: a molten cell with ctype k ≠ 0 uses `elDensity(k)` (the solid's density stands in for its liquid's).
- Display: a `molten` graphics function, a dark tint of the ctype's color under the existing glow. Readout: `label (ctype label)`.

### Picker

Three rows of about 32 px replace the 48 px bar, the canvas taking the rest of the height (the grid is sized at load, as now): the tabs, the selected entry and the search; the tab's entries; the status bar as today, without the buttons.

### Thermal shock

A solid with a crush and the two new properties `expansion` (linear, per °C) and `stiffness` (Young's modulus, GPa) cracks when a neighbor's temperature differs from its own by more than its shock limit ΔT = σ (1 − ν) / (E α): σ is its fracture toughness over √(π a) for a flaw size `a` shared by every material (the anchor of AC27, tens of micrometres), ν a fixed 0.25 (Poisson's ratio varies little), E and α its own. The limit is computed per row in TypeScript and packed as one row word. Phase 7 already computes each pair's difference; a solid whose difference passes its limit is marked, and phase 8 turns it into its crush target, after the temperature and pressure transitions, so it never cracks and melts in one dispatch. Metals (no crush, ductile) and diamond never crack; powders are already broken.

### Edge cases

- A cell from an older scene with bits 16–23 set: none exist (the bits were reserved and always zero); `Simulation.load` documents that a molten cell's ctype must name a row whose `above` becomes that molten element.
- A molten cell whose ctype has no `above` (a broken scene): validation of scenes in tests catches it; the GPU treats an unknown ctype as 0.
- A Heat or Cool stroke across a glass pane makes a sharp edge in temperature and can crack the glass along it, as uneven heating does to real glass.
- MOLTEN_METAL stays (ID 12): metals melt into it, and its `below` (METAL) is the ctype-0 default, so the `moltenMetal` scene is unchanged.
