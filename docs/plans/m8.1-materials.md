# M8.1 Materials and the element picker

Status: approved; next M8.1a.

## Goal

Twenty-one new everyday materials (metals from copper to tungsten, glass, brick, gravel, snow, sawdust, wax, liquid nitrogen, mercury…) that behave by the heat, phase-change, density and pressure rules the game already has, so those rules are tested on many more cases. Sand melts into glass, and every molten material cools back into what it was. A picker sorted into categories, with search, replaces the single scrolling button bar.

## Scope

In: the picker; `ctype` in `kind` bits 16–23; molten elements that remember what they set into (backlog I15); STONE and BRICK crushed into GRAVEL (STONE's stand-in SAND retires from that role); the 21 elements below, each with a scene test of its main behavior; a `?scene=materials` showcase; the budget; the M8 slices written into the roadmap.

Not in M8.1: anything that burns or explodes beyond what exists (M8.2), reactions between elements (M8.4), heavy gases (M8.3), electricity and light (M7: quartz, distilled water, glass refracting light, insulation blocking current), mercury's thermal expansion (backlog I22), conductivities above steel's (backlog D33: steel already sits at the cap of 1).

## Choices approved by the user

1. M8 comes before M7, in the slices below; the elements that need sparks or energy particles move to M7, those that need Newtonian gravity to M9.
2. The picker as in "Picker" below.
3. A blast crushes stone and brick into gravel, no longer into sand (changes M6 choice 3).
4. METAL's label becomes "Steel" (it is the steel-like metal among five).
5. Stickmen and fighters (TPT's player characters) are a backlog idea (I23), decided after M8: they are a game mechanic of their own, not a material.
6. The UI stays a prototype (no Claude Design canvas); the whole UI is redesigned once every feature exists.

## Terms

- **ctype**: a second element ID in a cell, `kind` bits 16–23 (TPT's name). 0 means none.
- **Molten element**: a row with `molten: true` (LAVA, MOLTEN_METAL). Its ctype is the element it sets into; with ctype 0 it sets into its own `below` (painted lava sets into stone, as now).
- **Crush**: a `pressureAbove` transition (M6.5).

## Elements

Each row takes its thresholds, density, conductivity rank and capacity from physical data, cited in its comment, as `src/elements.ts` does (AGENTS.md "Adding an element"). Crush pressures keep the physical order of strength: ice < glass < brick < stone; ceramic and diamond are never crushed.

| Key | State, category | Main behavior (the scene test) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GRAVEL | powder, Powders | What a blast leaves of stone and brick; melts into lava that sets into stone |
| SNOW | powder, Powders | What a blast leaves of ice; melts into water at 0 °C after its latent heat |
| SAWDUST | powder, Powders | Floats on water; flammable |
| BROKEN_GLASS | powder, Powders | What a blast leaves of glass; melts into molten glass |
| GLASS | solid, Solids | Sand melts into it; shatters under a weaker blast than stone |
| BRICK | solid, Solids | Crushed into gravel; lava does not melt it |
| CERAMIC | solid, Solids | Holds molten steel without melting; never crushed |
| DIAMOND | solid, Solids | Never melts or crushes (mimics no real material in that); conducts like steel |
| INSULATION | solid, Solids | Conductivity 0: heat never crosses it (a perfect insulator, not a real material) |
| WAX, MOLTEN_WAX | solid and liquid, Solids and Liquids | Melts near 60 °C, flows, sets again; flammable |
| SOLID_NITROGEN, LIQUID_NITROGEN, NITROGEN | solid, liquid, gas; Solids, Liquids, Gases | Liquid nitrogen freezes water it touches and boils off into nitrogen that rises |
| MERCURY, SOLID_MERCURY, MERCURY_VAPOR | liquid, solid, gas; Liquids, Metals, Gases | The densest liquid, under water and oil; freezes at −39 °C, boils at 357 °C |
| COPPER, GOLD, TITANIUM, TUNGSTEN | solid, Metals | Melt at their own temperature into molten metal that sets back into them; molten gold sinks under molten steel |

SAND gains `above` into LAVA with `setsInto: 'GLASS'`; STONE crushes into GRAVEL; ICE crushes into SNOW; METAL melts into MOLTEN_METAL with ctype METAL.

## Acceptance criteria

New test titles start with `M8.1 AC<n>:`.

Picker (M8.1c)

- AC1 `validateElements` SHALL reject a paintable row without a `category` from `CATEGORIES` or without a `description`, and a `toolbar` position used twice in one category (`tests/elements.test.ts`).
- AC2 The picker SHALL show one tab per category holding at least one entry, in `CATEGORIES` order, Tools last; every paintable element and tool is in exactly one tab (unit test on the pure functions in `src/toolbar.ts`).
- AC3 WHEN a tab is selected, THE SYSTEM SHALL show its entries in `toolbar` order, each with a color swatch, its label and its number key; WHEN 1–9 is pressed without Ctrl, Alt or Meta, THE SYSTEM SHALL select the n-th entry shown; with a modifier it SHALL do nothing (backlog B11) (unit test of the key mapping; browser check).
- AC4 WHEN the search field holds text, THE SYSTEM SHALL show the entries of every tab whose label contains it, ignoring case, in tab order; Enter selects the first, Escape clears it, `/` focuses it, and while it has focus no letter key triggers a shortcut (unit test of `searchEntries`; browser check).
- AC5 THE SYSTEM SHALL name the selected element or tool at all times, also when its tab is not shown (browser check).
- AC6 WHEN the pointer hovers, focuses or taps an entry, THE SYSTEM SHALL show its description in the `data-tip` tooltip, never a `title` (browser check).
- AC7 At 1280×720 and 1920×1080 no row of the picker scrolls sideways (browser check: `scrollWidth <= clientWidth` for each row).

Molten memory (M8.1d)

- AC8 THE SYSTEM SHALL keep a cell's ctype through falling, spreading, flight and landing, conduction and every helper that changes another field (`withLife`, `withMotion`, `withTemp`); `withType` and `retype` clear it (GPU test).
- AC9 WHEN a cell passes an `above` threshold into a molten element, THE SYSTEM SHALL write its `setsInto` (default: its own element) as the ctype; WHEN a molten cell with ctype k runs below k's `above.temp` by k's latent heat over the molten element's capacity, THE SYSTEM SHALL become k with ctype 0; WHEN its ctype is 0, its own `below` applies; the existing heat tests AC9–AC11 pass unchanged (GPU single cells).
- AC10 A sealed box holding every M8.1 transition, molten cells of several ctypes included, SHALL keep its total heat within the tolerance of heat AC14 over 2,000 supersteps (`tests/heat.test.ts`, `heatContent` and `latentOffsets` reading the ctype).
- AC11 A molten cell with ctype k SHALL move by k's density (molten gold sinks under molten steel; with ctype 0, its row's density) (GPU scene).
- AC12 Two molten cells of different ctypes at one temperature SHALL be drawn in different colors, and each SHALL still brighten as it heats (`tests/display.test.ts`).
- AC13 The pointer readout SHALL name what a molten cell sets into, for example "Lava (Glass)" (`tests/readout.test.ts`).
- AC14 `tests/block-locality.test.ts` SHALL generate a ctype for molten cells and pass.

Elements (M8.1e–g), `tests/materials.test.ts` unless named

- AC15 WHEN a gunpowder charge goes off among stone, brick, glass, ice, ceramic and diamond (the `air-crush` setup), THE SYSTEM SHALL turn stone and brick within the crush radius into gravel, glass into broken glass out to a larger radius than stone, ice into snow, and change no ceramic or diamond cell (`tests/air-crush.test.ts`, counts against measured values).
- AC16 WHEN sand runs past 1,713 °C by its latent width, THE SYSTEM SHALL turn it into molten glass that sets into GLASS, not sand; broken glass melts into the same molten glass (GPU single cells).
- AC17 Every `above` and `below` of the table SHALL be tested at its threshold by a test generated from the table, so each new row is covered: below the latent range it stays, past it it changes, the target is right (`tests/heat.test.ts`).
- AC18 WHEN sawdust is poured onto water, at least 95% of its cells SHALL end above the water's surface within a measured number of supersteps; sand poured the same way sinks (control).
- AC19 WHEN mercury, water, oil and sawdust are poured into one tank in any order, THE SYSTEM SHALL settle them in that order from the bottom (`tests/metrics.ts` layer order).
- AC20 WHEN liquid nitrogen is poured onto water, THE SYSTEM SHALL freeze some of the water into ice and boil the nitrogen into gas that rises above the water, with no liquid nitrogen left after a measured number of supersteps.
- AC21 WHEN wax lies on a plate held at 100 °C, THE SYSTEM SHALL melt it and the molten wax SHALL spread wider than the block it started as; molten wax poured onto ice SHALL set into wax.
- AC22 WHEN molten steel at 1,600 °C fills a ceramic cup and a tungsten cup, no cup cell SHALL change over 2,000 supersteps.
- AC23 WHEN lava touches a one-cell insulation wall, water on its far side SHALL stay within 0.5 °C of its start over 2,000 supersteps; behind a stone wall (control) it SHALL warm by at least a measured amount.
- AC24 Heat SHALL cross a diamond bar within 10% of the time it crosses a steel bar of the same size, and a diamond cell at 9,000 °C SHALL stay diamond.
- AC25 `?scene=materials` SHALL load a lab of the new materials (a lava pool under cups of each metal and ceramic, a tank of mercury, water, oil and sawdust, glass panes, brick and ice beside a gunpowder charge, a pool of liquid nitrogen over water) with every cell inside the grid (`tests/scenes.test.ts`).

## Design

### Table

- `ElementDef` gains `category?: Category` and `description?: string` (both required with `toolbar`, which becomes the position inside the category), `molten?: true`, and `Transition.setsInto?: string` (an `above` into a molten element only; default the row itself). `CATEGORIES` in `src/toolbar.ts`: Powders, Liquids, Gases, Solids, Metals, Explosives (FIRE, GUNPOWDER), Special, Life, then Tools (Heat, Cool, Wind). Descriptions are one sentence in our own words, never TPT's.
- Validation: a molten element has a `below`; a `setsInto` element has an `above` into the same molten element (that `above` is the pair the setting uses, so threshold and latent match by construction); the target-range check of a transition into a molten element uses the `setsInto` element's threshold.
- GPU row: `transitions` bits 24–31 hold the `setsInto` ID; `pad1` holds the molten flag. No new row word.

### ctype and the molten elements

- `cellCtype(c)` and `withCtype(c, k)` in `cellTypesWGSL`; `withType` and `retype` clear bits 16–23, the other helpers keep them; `makeCell` starts at ctype 0. A TS helper `kindWord(type, variant, ctype)` in `src/cell-fields.ts` for scenes and tests. Flyers carry the whole `kind` word, so nothing changes there.
- `cellTemp` and `effectiveTemp`: for a molten cell with ctype k ≠ 0, the lower threshold is `elAboveTemp(k)` and the latent width `elAboveWidth(k) * elHeatCapacity(k) / elHeatCapacity(t)`. Phase 8 compares the same values; a change into a molten element writes the `setsInto` ID. Conductivity and heat capacity stay the molten row's (lava's M6 tuning stays).
- `cellDensity`: a molten cell with ctype k ≠ 0 uses `elDensity(k)` (the solid's density stands in for its liquid's).
- Display: a `molten` graphics function, a dark tint of the ctype's color under the existing glow. Readout: `label (ctype label)`.

### Picker

Three rows of about 32 px replace the 48 px bar, the canvas taking the rest of the height (the grid is sized at load, as now): the tabs, the selected entry and the search; the tab's entries; the status bar as today, without the buttons.

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Powders  Liquids  Gases  Solids  [Metals]  Explosives  Tools      Painting: Gold   🔍 Search (/) │
│ [■ Steel 1] [■ Copper 2] [■ Gold 3] [■ Titanium 4] [■ Tungsten 5]                        │
│ Gold · 412 °C · pressure +0.02 │ Brush 10 │ 60 FPS │ 3.4M cells │ ‹ 1x › │ Heat view │ Air view │ Clear │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Spacing and the order of the status items are the implementer's, under AC7. The selected entry has a border and bold label besides its color (the user is red-green colorblind). Pure functions in `src/toolbar.ts` (`pickerTabs`, `searchEntries`, `entryForKey`) carry the logic the unit tests check; `main.ts` wires the DOM.

### Edge cases

- A cell from an older scene with bits 16–23 set: none exist (the bits were reserved and always zero); `Simulation.load` documents that a molten cell's ctype must name a row whose `above` becomes that molten element.
- A molten cell whose ctype has no `above` (a broken scene): validation of scenes in tests catches it; the GPU treats an unknown ctype as 0.
- MOLTEN_METAL stays (ID 12): metals melt into it, and its `below` (METAL) is the ctype-0 default, so the `moltenMetal` scene is unchanged.

## M8 slices

Written into `docs/roadmap.md` by M8.1a. Each slice gets its own plan when it starts.

- **M8** Element catalogue: The Powder Toy's elements that need only what the game simulates before M7 (heat, fire, air and pressure), slice by slice. Each models a real material where one exists. Done when every slice is done: each element has a scene test of its main behavior, and the frame budget holds.
  - **M8.1** Materials and the element picker (this plan).
  - **M8.2** Fire and explosives: slow burning (wood, coal, fuse, ignition cord), dust that burns in a flash, flammable gas, plasma, cold flame, pressure-set explosives (C-4, nitroglycerin, diesel), TNT that goes off as a whole (backlog I18), bombs, thermite, fireworks, a cold-set explosive, and stored energy (uranium heats under pressure, vibranium, the singularity).
  - **M8.3** Air, gases and force: heavy gases that sink (carbon dioxide, dry ice), gases that expand when heated or cool when compressed (Boyle gas, refrigerant), vents and vacuum, accelerators and decelerators, repellers, anti-air dust, glow that lights up under pressure, goo, concrete, dust colored by its speed, a wall that opens under pressure.
  - **M8.4** Chemistry: reactions between elements in contact: salt dissolves into salt water, clay and water make paste that bakes into brick, acid dissolves and base neutralizes it, hydrogen burns with oxygen into water (oxygen and liquid oxygen), lithium and rubidium explode in water, iron rusts into broken metal, platinum speeds reactions, soda water fizzes, virus spreads and soap cures it, antimatter annihilates, sponge and gel soak up water, yeast grows, freeze powder.
  - **M8.5** Life: Life-like and Generations rules (Conway's Life and its variants, TPT's set of about 24, rules from LifeWiki's public catalogue) on painted cells, in a pass of their own outside the Margolus CA.
  - **M8.6** Special and growing: clone, breakable clone, converter, void, warp, TRON, plants that drink water and grow, vines, seeds that grow trees, bizarre (it melts when cold).
  - **M8.7** Portals and pipes: portals that carry what enters one to another of the same channel, pipes that carry particles along their length.
- Added to **M7**: "It also brings the elements that need sparks or energy particles: TPT's electronics, powered materials and sensors, beams, pistons and frames, the radioactive elements that emit particles, quartz, filter, distilled water, noble gas, fog and rime, resist, shield, lightning and thunder."
- Added to **M9**: "And the elements that need it: black and white holes, the gravity bomb, gravitons, the gravity pump."

- **D33** (M8) Conductivity is a share per exchange capped at 1, and steel already sits at the cap, so copper, gold, tungsten and diamond conduct no faster than steel. Re-ranking steel lower would retune M5's heat tests (metal bars, the `thermal` bench). `src/elements.ts`
- **I22** (M8) Mercury's volume grows with its temperature in TPT; here it does not. `src/elements.ts` MERCURY
- **I23** (M8) Stickmen and fighters, TPT's player characters: a figure steered with the keyboard that walks, carries an element and dies in lava or acid, and fighters that hunt it. A game mechanic of its own (controls, a body that is not a cell); decide after M8 whether it becomes a milestone.
