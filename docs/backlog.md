# Backlog

IDs are stable: B = bug, D = tech debt, I = idea.

## Bugs

- **B7** Touch devices cannot erase or change brush size (right click and wheel only), and `pointercancel` is not handled, so a cancelled touch can leave drawing stuck on. `src/input.ts:50-73`
- **B8** Brush ring states (erase red, replace amber, draw white) differ by color only, which is unreadable with red-green colorblindness; add a second cue (dashed ring, icon, label). `src/shaders/display.wgsl.ts` `fs_main`
- **B10** No per-grain color variation: `variant` is generated and stored, but the display shader ignores it (the WebGL version shaded sand, wall and water by variant until commit e6a38a9). `src/shaders/display.wgsl.ts` `fs_main`
- **B11** Number keys ignore modifiers, so Ctrl+1 (browser tab switch) also selects a tool. `src/main.ts` keydown handler

## Tech debt

- **D10** Move to TypeScript 7 (the native port) once typescript-eslint supports it: 8.x accepts `typescript <6.1`, so the project is on 6.0.x.
- **D11** The GPU test suites (`tests/simulation.test.ts`, `tests/hash.test.ts`, `tests/gpu.test.ts`) skip themselves on machines without a GPU, which includes the GitHub Actions runners, so CI only runs the unit tests. A software adapter on the runner (for example Dawn with SwiftShader) would let CI run them.
- **D12** ESLint uses typescript-eslint's recommended type-checked rules (`eslint.config.js`), not the `create-corniflex` template (`@antfu/eslint-config`).
- **D24** A liquid at rest still rewrites its cells: a cell facing a non-empty neighbor turns around every dispatch (its direction is in `life`), so the words of a still pool keep changing. It defeats the "write back only what changed" saving of `storeChanged` for a resting pool; if that lever is wanted, turn only cells that have empty space beside them or above. `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `spreadRow`
- **D28** The flyer buffers swap by a parity kept in JS and advanced when a superstep is recorded: a frame that is recorded but never runs (a validation error, `getCurrentTexture()` throwing after `simulate`) with an odd number of supersteps leaves the next frame reading the wrong buffer, so grains are lost or doubled. Keep the parity on the GPU. `src/flyers.ts` `parity`
- **D29** A flyer that can find no free cell (walled in by painting, nothing free within the search) stays in the list for good and is invisible, because the overlay only draws over empty cells; `jammed` counts failures, not consecutive ones. The flight walk can also step past its end cell when both end coordinates are exact integers and rounding breaks a tie, and then claims a cell it did not check. `src/shaders/flyers.wgsl.ts` `claimNearby`, `flightWGSL`. Measured case: the `mound` scene (AC27) leaves 13 to 17 of 7,300 flyers jammed for good.
- **D31** Every pass binds every cell field, and the CA loads and stores all of them for each active block: with four fields, loading and storing them cost about 0.5 ms for four dispatches, and the fifth (temperature) added about a quarter to the CA's time (0.2 ms per 1x frame on the busy `mix75` and `full100` bench scenes), so each new field (`ctype`, `tmp`) adds its share to every pass. Heat's conduction and phase changes cost +0.52 ms on `mix75` and +0.40 on `full100` per 1x frame, inside their 1 ms share; levers if more is needed: to load `velocity` and `remainder` only for cells whose state falls and `temp` only for cells that hold heat (every path that writes a cell that holds no heat must then write 0 into the skipped words), and to skip a block whose heat is at rest (all temperatures equal and inside their thresholds). `CellFields.subset` and `cellBindingsWGSL(access, names)` bind a pass to a list of fields (binding = field index); the flyer flight and retry passes use them and bind `kind` only. The CA, the brush, the display, the launch scatter and the landings still bind every field: give them a subset when they read less than all of it. `src/cell-field-buffers.ts` `subset`, `src/shaders/cell.wgsl.ts` `cellBindingsWGSL`, `src/flyers.ts`
- **D32** (M7) The flyer passes that carry whole cells (launch scatter, flight, retry, both landings) bind three buffers of their own plus one per cell field: with five fields that is the default limit of 8 storage buffers per stage, which the game and the tests request and which some adapters do not exceed (the RTX 4080 offers 16). A sixth field fails pipeline creation there. Keep `ctype` (the likely next field) in `kind`'s reserved bits, or bind fewer buffers in those passes (D31), before adding one. `src/flyers.ts`, `src/cell-fields.ts`
- **D17** Page load stalls 2–4 s before the first frames run (seen in the browser at 1920×898 before the liquids moved to CA rules). In Node, creating the `Simulation` takes about 0.5–0.8 s the first time (compiling the pipelines; the CA's per-slot variables doubled its share) and 2–9 ms after (`npm run bench`), so profile the load in the browser again before optimizing; if it still stalls, create the pipelines with `createComputePipelineAsync` and show a loading state. `src/simulation.ts`

## Ideas

- **I10** Bigger scenes for `?scene=`: `uTube` (68×80) and `damBreak` (200×60) are too small to enjoy or to judge whether the physics looks right; they are fine as tests. `src/scenes.ts`
- **I11** A separate realistic-physics sandbox built from the FLIP liquids that M4 replaced with CA rules, only worth starting if its hook is the interplay of a few deep materials (heat boiling water into steam that expands, pushes and condenses; lava flashing water to steam; burning oil; explosions throwing water; objects that float or sink), not water alone. Desktop GPU first; liquids at 2 pixels per cell with a smooth rendered surface, foam and spray; gases as a grid-only solve on a coarse grid. Start from the last CorniSand commit that has `src/fluid.ts`, and carry over the FLIP items M4 deleted from this backlog (B12, D13–D15, D18–D22, I9) as its known issues.
- **I13** (M6) Flyers in flight are outside the grid, so only the brush's erase and replace reach them: pressure acts on grid cells only, and a sand stream falling fast enough to fly is out of reach of whatever acts on the grid. Pressure and explosions must also push the flyers in their area. `src/shaders/flyers.wgsl.ts`
- **I14** A flyer is blocked by any non-empty cell, gases included, so smoke in its path stops it where it is. Real drag from gases would need the flyer to slow down in them instead. `src/shaders/flyers.wgsl.ts` `isFree`
- **I15** (M8) One LAVA that remembers the element it melted from (TPT's `ctype`, which fits in `kind`'s reserved bits 16–23) instead of one molten element per meltable material, once more materials melt than stone and metal (glass from sand, brick…). `src/elements.ts`
- **I16** SMOKE is a placeholder: one gas for whatever burns, with stand-in heat values. Define the smoke mechanic: smoke by what burns and how hot, how it cools and disperses (likely with M6's air), what it leaves behind. `src/elements.ts`
- **I17** A flyer is drawn over an empty cell from its `kind` word only, with that cell's meaningless `temp`, so a flying hot cell has no glow and takes its element color in the heat view until it lands. Give the overlay the flyer's `temp` word too. `src/flyers.ts` `recordOverlay`, `src/shaders/display.wgsl.ts`
- **I18** (M6) A lit gunpowder charge burns only part of itself: the flame spreads one cell per dispatch and each burning cell's burst throws its neighbours away first (16 of 48 cells burned in the open, about 24 of 48 under sand in the `explosion` scene; the rest lands as unburnt powder). A faster burn (the flame crossing a connected charge in one dispatch) or a smaller burst per cell with a larger charge would make a bare pile of powder explode as a whole. `src/elements.ts` GUNPOWDER, `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` phase 6
- **I19** (M6) Hot air pushes sand: next to a big hot plate (the `plume` scene's 64x4 plate at 1,000 °C) the plume's pressure deficit keeps sand piles creeping toward it for thousands of supersteps (the push on sand is about 10 cells per dispatch squared per unit of pressure gradient). Lower `AIR.buoyancy` and raise `AIR.heatCapacity` to keep the cooling, or add a dead band below which the pressure gradient does not push matter. The `mound` scene shows it too: grains that landed loose on the hot floor keep sliding for about 10 s after the blast (2.0% of the sand moves per 60 supersteps at 1,200 supersteps after the ignition, 0.9% at 2,400). Judge at the M6.6 gate. `src/air.ts`, `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `velocityRule`
- **I20** (M6) A blast crushes a stone mass only from its surface: a stone cell reads the pressure of the air cell it is in, and an air cell that is half solid or more is closed to the air, so a wall of stone 3 or more cells thick takes no pressure inside it and each crushed layer (it becomes sand) opens the next. Stone in thin lines or single cells is crushed at the air's pressure as measured (`tests/air-crush.test.ts`). If the playtest finds thick walls too tough, let a closed air cell take the mean pressure of its open neighbors. `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `transitionCell`, `src/shaders/air.wgsl.ts` `faceGate`
- **I12** Choose a license for CorniSand. With no license file nobody may reuse the code; a permissive license (MIT, Apache-2.0) lets others reuse it without requiring them, or this project, to publish source. Decide before accepting outside contributions.
