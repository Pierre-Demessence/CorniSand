# Backlog

IDs are stable: B = bug, D = tech debt, I = idea.

## Bugs

- **B7** Touch devices cannot erase or change brush size (right click and wheel only), and `pointercancel` is not handled, so a cancelled touch can leave drawing stuck on. `src/input.ts:50-73`
- **B8** Brush ring states (erase red, replace amber, draw white) differ by color only, which is unreadable with red-green colorblindness; add a second cue (dashed ring, icon, label). `src/shaders/display.wgsl.ts:85-101`
- **B9** Toolbar does not wrap or scroll, so items are cut off on narrow screens. `index.html:30-45`
- **B10** No per-grain color variation: `variant` is generated and stored, but the display shader ignores it (the WebGL version shaded sand, wall and water by variant until commit e6a38a9). `src/shaders/display.wgsl.ts:71-82`
- **B11** Number keys ignore modifiers, so Ctrl+1 (browser tab switch) also selects a tool. `src/main.ts:112-115`
- **B12** Reported, not reproduced: painting oil is said to surround it with a ring of water, a thin layer of water staying on top of the oil after it lands, and a layer that keeps the same thickness however much oil is added. Four GPU scenes (oil painted in the air over a settled pool, a poured stream of water then oil, oil dragged across the surface, oil on bare floor; 200×160 to 400×400 grids, tiles and `activeTiles: false` identical) gave pure oil cells, water and oil cell counts equal to the particle counts / 4 and `maxShortfall` 0. The padding rings of the rasterization (`src/shaders/fluid-raster.wgsl.ts` `padRing`) would draw water around oil only while the particles fill fewer cells than the volume needs; get the brush size, speed, whether Shift was held and the grid size from the report, and look at `maxShortfall` in that session.

## Tech debt

- **D10** Move to TypeScript 7 (the native port) once typescript-eslint supports it: 8.x accepts `typescript <6.1`, so the project is on 6.0.x.
- **D11** The GPU test suites (`tests/simulation.test.ts`, `tests/hash.test.ts`) skip themselves on machines without a GPU, which includes the GitHub Actions runners, so CI only runs the unit tests. A software adapter on the runner (for example Dawn with SwiftShader) would let CI run them.
- **D12** ESLint uses typescript-eslint's recommended type-checked rules (`eslint.config.js`), not the `create-corniflex` template (`@antfu/eslint-config`).
- **D13** The CPU liquid acceptance suite (`tests/liquids-*.test.ts`) takes about 100 s wall (260 s summed) on the dev machine and runs on CI too; shrink the large scenes or step counts, or run it on demand, if CI time matters.
- **D14** FLIP particles keep moving at rest: about 19% exceed 0.3 cells/step in the settled large U-tube on the CPU reference, 8–17% on the GPU (the screen-size `deepPool` and `uTubeHuge` end with none). The ranked cell output hides it, but it needs damping before sand sinks through water. `tests/cpu/flip.ts` `gridToParticles`
- **D15** FLIP at `k = 2` fails AC2, AC5, AC6 and AC11 (stray cells over the surface, oil mixing into water) on the CPU reference; not run on the GPU. On the GPU the pressure solve is about 18.5 of 49 ms of a 1x frame at 2560×1340 (multigrid with active tiles), so `k = 2` is a cost lever of at most that size; see D22. `tests/cpu/flip.ts`
- **D22** Liquids are slower than the 60 fps target at 2560×1440 (AC14 of the finished M1 plan): 49 ms of GPU pass time and 52 ms of span for a 1x frame at 2560×1340 with a quarter of the tank water (`npm run bench`), and 43 fps measured in the browser at 1920×897 with 427k particles (a quarter of the screen). Cost-neutral levers are used up (multigrid, active tiles). Remaining: the playing-field size (I10), the particle scatter (D21), fewer dispatches (about 300 per frame, ~0.04 ms idle each), and the look-changing ones: fluid update every second superstep (about half the frame), `k = 2` (D15), a coarser grid.
- **D21** The particle-to-grid scatter is 15 of 49 ms of a 1x frame at 2560×1340 after active tiles (16 global atomics per particle). A workgroup-shared accumulation only helps when the particles of a workgroup are neighbours in space, which they are not (spawn order, then compaction order); sort or bucket the particles by cell first, or fuse the small passes (about 300 dispatches a frame, ~0.04 ms idle each). `src/shaders/fluid-particles.wgsl.ts` `particlesToGrid`, `the finished plan, found with `git log --all -- docs/plans/physics-foundation.md`` ("M1.5c measurements")
- **D19** Single-cell holes in deep calm water: with the density correction lowered to 0.1 (`FLUID.stiffness`, `pull`) the half-size `deepPool` (256×600) reaches its level with no shortfall, but at rest an interior cell with 2 particles among cells of 4–6 is not chosen by the rasterization (it ranks by crowdedness), so `flatness` exceeds 10 in about 80% of steps. A higher `pull` overdrives deep water instead. Candidate fixes, untested: rank interior cells higher in `rasterKeys`, or bound the correction per column. The full-size `deepPool` on the GPU (512×1200) still reaches flatness 1 (M1.5c.3d), so the holes have not cost an acceptance test; the half-size CPU pool shows them. `the finished plan, found with `git log --all -- docs/plans/physics-foundation.md`` ("M1.5c measurements", E4), `tests/cpu/flip.ts` `rasterKeys`
- **D20** The GPU keeps the plain red-black sweep solver (`FLUID.solver = 'sor'`, `Fluid`'s `sweeps` pass group) only as the bench baseline of the multigrid solve; delete it with the `solver` option and the `iterations` option once nothing compares against it. `src/fluid.ts`
- **D18** The oil/water interface flickers: at rest in `oilOnWater` a lone cell is inverted (water inside the oil layer or the reverse) for single steps, because water and oil particles overlap in a band about one cell wide. After step 1480 the GPU shows one in 8 of 60 steps and the CPU in 1 of 60 (one cell in that window, 3 at step 200); AC11 therefore checks the median over 100 steps. Smoothing it goes with D14. `tests/liquid-acceptance.ts` `expectOilAboveWater`
- **D17** Page load stalls 2–4 s before the first frames run (seen in the browser at 1920×898 on `main`). In Node, creating the `Simulation` at that size takes about 0.8 s the first time (pipelines) and nothing measurable after, so pipeline compilation explains at most a part of it; profile the load in the browser first, then create the pipelines with `createComputePipelineAsync` and show a loading state. `src/simulation.ts`, `src/fluid.ts`

## Ideas

- **I10** A smaller playing field: cap the grid at about 1M cells, sometimes 500k (today it is one cell per CSS pixel, 1.7M at 1920×897), to buy frame rate for the liquids; probably a cells-per-pixel scale of 2 or a limit picked by the player. Also bigger scenes for `?scene=`: `uTube` (68×80) and `damBreak` (200×60) are too small to enjoy or to judge whether the physics looks right; they are fine as tests. `src/grid.ts`, `src/scenes.ts`

- **I8** Debug overlay for the fluid fields of M1 (velocity, pressure), once they exist.
- **I9** Liquid falls at most 1 cell per step (the FLIP velocity clamp), half the speed of falling sand; a swept wall test in the particle move would allow a clamp of 2 without particles crossing thin walls. `tests/cpu/flip.ts` `gridToParticles`
