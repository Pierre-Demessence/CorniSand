## Bugs

- **B7** Touch devices cannot erase or change brush size (right click and wheel only), and `pointercancel` is not handled, so a cancelled touch can leave drawing stuck on. `src/input.ts:50-73`
- **B8** Brush ring states (erase red, replace amber, draw white) differ by color only, which is unreadable with red-green colorblindness; add a second cue (dashed ring, icon, label). `src/shaders/display.wgsl.ts` `fs_main`
- **B14** A tall pour of lava can still leave a few stone cells hanging in mid-air: a cell that spreads sideways in the stream moves too slowly to skip the air exchange (`AIR.stillSpeed`), sets, and a solid never moves (4 stone cells above row 40 in AC28's 380-cell pour; a sparse column of them in a 700-cell pour, `tests/air-heat.test.ts`). A liquid that would set while unsupported could wait until it rests on something. `src/shaders/air.wgsl.ts` `airExchangeWGSL`, `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `transitionCell`
- **B15** (M12) A sealed cup of water with a melting ice cube evens out in about an hour, where a real cup mixes in a minute or so: the cold meltwater (0 °C, 999.8 kg/m³) is denser than the 20 °C water (998.2) and should sink, but in `iceCup` (a full 25×40 cup, 100 ice in 900 water) the temperatures still span 8.9 to 11.1 °C 5 min after the last ice melts (mean 10.01 °C, so the heat balance is right). Cause not found: the `convect` chance for a 0.16% density difference may be too small to stir a cup this size, or the full walled cup leaves it no room to circulate. `src/physics.ts` `convectionChance`, `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `convect`, `tests/scenarios-heat.test.ts` `iceCup`
- **B16** (M12) A pool of lava open to still air hardly cools at its surface: in `lavaCrust` (a pool 64 wide and 80 deep at 1,300 °C under 34 rows of air) the top cells lose 0.3 K in 2 minutes and no STONE forms in 9 minutes (3 seeds), where a 16×16 steel bar at 1,000 °C loses 85 K in 125 s through its four sides (`redHotBar`). Inferred cause, not isolated: a liquid cell trades heat with the air cell it stands in (`airOf`), not with the air above it, so the 4×4 air cells over the pool sit at the pool's temperature and the air column above fills with hot air (400 to 1,000 °C up to 20 cells above the surface) that the plume and the diffusion remove too slowly. `src/shaders/air.wgsl.ts` `airExchangeWGSL`, `tests/scenarios-heat.test.ts` `lavaCrust`
- **B17** (M12) Sand runs through an outlet in proportion to its width, where real grains follow Beverloo's law (the rate grows as the width to the power 3/2, 2D): in `hourglass` (a silo 64 wide, 6 widths of sand) widening the outlet from 8 to 16 cells multiplies the rate by 1.84 (1.84, 1.84 and 1.86 on seeds 1 to 3), real grains by 2.83 to 3.56; a liquid of sand's density does 1.90. The rate itself is steady (within 1% of its mean from 6 to 1.5 silo widths). Cause not found; inferred: grains above the outlet gain speed over a length that does not grow with the outlet, where a real free-fall arch grows with it. `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `velocityRule`, `tests/scenarios-flow.test.ts` `hourglass`
- **B18** (M12) A pile built by a stream of sand grows steeper with its size and ends up steeper than sand's angle of repose: in `hourglass` the pile under the outlet, plugged and settled, stands at 43 to 44 degrees after 18,432 cells (42.9, 42.6 and 43.5 on seeds 1 to 3 at outlet 8; 43.5, 43.3 and 43.6 at 16), where the row says 34 and `sandPile` (a pour from a point, 4,000 grains) measures 34. Through the outlet of 8 the same pile measures 20 degrees after 948 cells, 36 after 2,508 and 41 after 4,968 (seed 1). `REPOSE_FIT` was fitted on piles of a few thousand grains. Cause not found. `src/physics.ts` `REPOSE_FIT`, `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `velocityRule`, `tests/scenarios-flow.test.ts` `hourglass`
- **B19** The speed is per frame, not per second: every frame runs `SPEED_STEPS` supersteps whatever its duration, so 1x is real time only on a 60 Hz display with no dropped frame (4 supersteps per frame is 240 per second); a 144 Hz display runs 1x at 2.4 times real time and a slow frame slows the game down. A fixed-timestep loop (supersteps owed from the elapsed time, capped per frame) would make the speed mean what its label says; `DISPATCHES_PER_SECOND` in `src/physics.ts` documents the 60 Hz assumption. `src/main.ts` `animate`, `src/physics.ts`

## Tech debt

- **D10** Move to TypeScript 7 (the native port) once typescript-eslint supports it: 8.x accepts `typescript <6.1`, so the project is on 6.0.x.
- **D11** The GPU test suites (`tests/simulation.test.ts`, `tests/hash.test.ts`, `tests/gpu.test.ts`) skip themselves on machines without a GPU, which includes the GitHub Actions runners, so CI only runs the unit tests. A software adapter on the runner (for example Dawn with SwiftShader) would let CI run them.
- **D12** ESLint uses typescript-eslint's recommended type-checked rules (`eslint.config.js`), not the `create-corniflex` template (`@antfu/eslint-config`).
- **D24** A liquid at rest still rewrites its cells: a cell facing a non-empty neighbor turns around every dispatch (its direction is in `life`), so the words of a still pool keep changing. It defeats the "write back only what changed" saving of `storeChanged` for a resting pool; if that lever is wanted, turn only cells that have empty space beside them or above. `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `spreadRow`
- **D28** The flyer buffers swap by a parity kept in JS and advanced when a superstep is recorded: a frame that is recorded but never runs (a validation error, `getCurrentTexture()` throwing after `simulate`) with an odd number of supersteps leaves the next frame reading the wrong buffer, so grains are lost or doubled. Keep the parity on the GPU. `src/flyers.ts` `parity`
- **D29** A flyer that can find no free cell (walled in by painting, nothing free within the search) stays in the list for good and is invisible, because the overlay only draws over empty cells; `jammed` counts failures, not consecutive ones. The flight walk can also step past its end cell when both end coordinates are exact integers and rounding breaks a tie, and then claims a cell it did not check. `src/shaders/flyers.wgsl.ts` `claimNearby`, `flightWGSL`. Measured case: the `mound` scene (AC27) leaves 13 to 17 of 7,300 flyers jammed for good.
- **D31** Every pass binds every cell field, and the CA loads and stores all of them for each active block: with four fields, loading and storing them cost about 0.5 ms for four dispatches, and the fifth (temperature) added about a quarter to the CA's time (0.2 ms per 1x frame on the busy `mix75` and `full100` bench scenes), so each new field (`ctype`, `tmp`) adds its share to every pass. Heat's conduction and phase changes cost +0.52 ms on `mix75` and +0.40 on `full100` per 1x frame, inside their 1 ms share; levers if more is needed: to load `velocity` and `remainder` only for cells whose state falls and `temp` only for cells that hold heat (every path that writes a cell that holds no heat must then write 0 into the skipped words), and to skip a block whose heat is at rest (all temperatures equal and inside their thresholds). `CellFields.subset` and `cellBindingsWGSL(access, names)` bind a pass to a list of fields (binding = field index); the flyer flight and retry passes use them and bind `kind` only. The air's exchange pass binds the three fields it reads. The CA, the brush, the display, the launch scatter and the landings still bind every field: give them a subset when they read less than all of it. `src/cell-field-buffers.ts` `subset`, `src/shaders/cell.wgsl.ts` `cellBindingsWGSL`, `src/flyers.ts`
- **D32** (M7) The flyer passes that carry whole cells (launch scatter, flight, retry, both landings) bind three buffers of their own plus one per cell field: with five fields that is the default limit of 8 storage buffers per stage, which the game and the tests request and which some adapters do not exceed (the RTX 4080 offers 16). The CA is at the limit too with air (five fields, the launch counts, the air state and the burst buffer). A sixth field fails pipeline creation there. Keep `ctype` (the likely next field) in `kind`'s reserved bits, or bind fewer buffers in those passes (D31), before adding one. `src/flyers.ts`, `src/cell-fields.ts`
- **D33** (M6) The air's speeds are not real-scale: a pressure wave moves one cell per dispatch (0.65 m/s on the physical scale, about 500× slower than sound), so blasts, wind and plumes are slowed; the air's buoyancy stays tuned. The real-world scenario `soundSpeed` (a bang crosses the 3.5 m world in 10 ms) is out of reach for that reason. `src/air.ts`
- **D34** (M8) Two different elements are ordered by their nominal densities whatever their temperatures (`canDisplace`, `canRiseThrough`); only the cells of one element use the density at their temperatures (`convect`). Checking both at the two temperatures slowed the CA by about 15%, and no pair of the table crosses over. A pair that does (M8.1's materials) needs the temperature-aware compare, made cheap. `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts`
- **D35** (M8) Gases do not sort among themselves: a gas rises or sinks only against the air (empty space), fire or a liquid, so a heavy gas under a lighter one, or hot smoke under cooler smoke, stays where it is. A gas-gas swap by density (as `convect` does for liquids) would need the temperature-aware compare of D34. `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `buoyancy`
- **D36** (M8) The two CA passes cost 0.4 to 0.6 ms more per 1x frame on the busy bench scenes since M8.8 (`mix75` 2.10 to 2.53 ms, `full100` 1.90 to 2.29, `thermal` 2.29 to 2.85; ca0 and ca1 of `thermal` 0.86 and 0.88 to 0.96 and 0.98 ms), accepted at M8.8j. The cause is not isolated; candidates (inferred): the table row growing from 96 to 144 bytes, the density columns read in `convect`, the viscosity gate and the repose slide. A profiling pass (one candidate removed at a time under `npm run bench`) would show which is worth recovering; judge it once M8.1 and M8.2 show how much of the 4 ms engine budget is used. `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts`, `src/element-table.ts`
- **D17** Page load stalls 2–4 s before the first frames run (seen in the browser at 1920×898 before the liquids moved to CA rules). In Node, creating the `Simulation` takes about 0.5–0.8 s the first time (compiling the pipelines; the CA's per-slot variables doubled its share) and 2–9 ms after (`npm run bench`), so profile the load in the browser again before optimizing; if it still stalls, create the pipelines with `createComputePipelineAsync` and show a loading state. `src/simulation.ts`
- **D37** (M10) Renderer costs left from M11's review, inside the 1 ms budget: `shimmerShift` computes the pressure gradient (four air samples) at every pixel, even over still air; the coverage pass reads each cell's `kind` up to four times and walks its 16 cells twice; `liquidDepth` loops up to 32 texels per liquid pixel, which a running sum per column written by the coverage pass would replace. `tests/look.test.ts` builds a new `Display` (every renderer pipeline) per render, which slows the suite. `src/shaders/display.wgsl.ts`, `src/shaders/glow.wgsl.ts`, `tests/look.test.ts`
- **D38** The Scenes menu renderer has no automated test (keyboard handling, the Escape stages, the current marking, the hotkey guard in `main.ts`): the project has no DOM test environment, and jsdom or happy-dom would be a new dev dependency, or a small Playwright script kept in the repo. `src/scene-menu.ts` `renderSceneMenu`
- **D39** The Scenes menu's accessibility: the listbox holds rows with chip buttons beside the option (a listbox should hold options only), and the dialog says `aria-modal` while the toolbar behind it stays clickable so the Scenes button can toggle. `src/scene-menu.ts` `buildRow`, `index.html` `.scene-menu`
- **D40** (M12) Heat crosses a good conductor faster than its data say: a block's pairs exchange in two sweeps one after the other, which makes the effective diffusivity alpha (1 + r) for r = `PAIR_TIME` alpha / `CELL_SIZE`² (the share of its difference a pair closes per superstep, 0.03 for steel, 0.22 to 0.29 for aluminium, copper and gold). The `metalBars` bars of aluminium, copper and gold relax 24, 31 and 36% faster than the ratio of their diffusivities to steel's (target within 15%; a CPU model of the sweeps gives the same numbers, `tests/materials.test.ts`), and any other conductor with r over 0.15 is off the same way; diamond's pair also hits the cap of `heatShare`, so its bar relaxes at the grid's fastest pace (about 30 times steel, the data say 85). A candidate fix is to scale the share by 1 / (1 + r) in `heatShare` and `exchangeHeat` (`src/heat.ts`, `src/shaders/heat.wgsl.ts`) and re-measure the heat tests; the scenarios `metalBars-aluminium`, `-copper` and `-gold` then pass and lose their `waitsFor`.

## Ideas

- **I10** Bigger scenes for `?scene=`: `uTube` (68×80) and `damBreak` (200×60) are too small to enjoy or to judge whether the physics looks right; they are fine as tests. `src/scenes.ts`
- **I11** A separate realistic-physics sandbox built from the FLIP liquids that M4 replaced with CA rules, only worth starting if its hook is the interplay of a few deep materials (heat boiling water into steam that expands, pushes and condenses; lava flashing water to steam; burning oil; explosions throwing water; objects that float or sink), not water alone. Desktop GPU first; liquids at 2 pixels per cell with a smooth rendered surface, foam and spray; gases as a grid-only solve on a coarse grid. Start from the last CorniSand commit that has `src/fluid.ts`, and carry over the FLIP items M4 deleted from this backlog (B12, D13–D15, D18–D22, I9) as its known issues.
- **I14** A flyer is blocked by any non-empty cell, gases included, so smoke in its path stops it where it is. Real drag from gases would need the flyer to slow down in them instead. `src/shaders/flyers.wgsl.ts` `isFree`
- **I16** SMOKE is a placeholder: one gas for whatever burns, with stand-in heat values. Define the smoke mechanic: smoke by what burns and how hot, how it cools and disperses (the air carries smoke sideways and cools it, but a hot plume does not lift it faster than it rises alone), what it leaves behind. `src/elements.ts`
- **I18** A lit gunpowder charge burns only part of itself: the flame spreads one cell per dispatch and each burning cell's burst throws its neighbours away first (16 of 48 cells burned in the open, about 24 of 48 under sand in the `explosion` scene; the rest lands as unburnt powder). A faster burn (the flame crossing a connected charge in one dispatch) or a smaller burst per cell with a larger charge would make a bare pile of powder explode as a whole. `src/elements.ts` GUNPOWDER, `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` phase 6
- **I19** Hot air pushes sand: next to a big hot plate (the `plume` scene's 64x4 plate at 1,000 °C) the plume's pressure deficit keeps sand piles creeping toward it for thousands of supersteps (the push on sand is about 10 cells per dispatch squared per unit of pressure gradient). Lower `AIR.buoyancy` (the air's heat capacity is physical, M8.8d), or add a dead band below which the pressure gradient does not push matter. The `mound` scene shows it too: grains that landed loose on the hot floor keep sliding for about 10 s after the blast (2.0% of the sand moves per 60 supersteps at 1,200 supersteps after the ignition, 0.9% at 2,400). `src/air.ts`, `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `velocityRule`
- **I21** Moving matter does not drag the air: a falling sand stream or a poured liquid pulls no air down with it, and a thrown grain leaves no wake. Matter could add to the air's velocity along its own (a gather in the mask pass, which already counts the matter share of each air cell). `src/shaders/air.wgsl.ts` `airMaskWGSL`
- **I22** (M12) Nothing changes volume: a cell is one unit of matter in every state, so mercury does not swell when heated (TPT's does), water does not grow 9% as it freezes, and boiling stays a burst. A conserved amount of matter per cell, packed into the fewest cells that hold it, would allow it, if each cell's heat capacity follows its amount; decide on a thermometer scene once M8.1 adds mercury. The real-world scenario `boilingVolume` (a boiling puddle fills 1,700 times its volume with steam) is out of reach until then: a 10×10 puddle would make 170,000 cells. `docs/research/volume-changes.md`
- **I23** (M8) Stickmen and fighters, TPT's player characters: a figure steered with the keyboard that walks, carries an element and dies in lava or acid, and fighters that hunt it. A game mechanic of its own (controls, a body that is not a cell); decide after M8 whether it becomes a milestone.
- **I24** (M8) Viscosity is constant per element; real lava and molten glass stiffen by orders of magnitude as they cool toward setting. `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `spreadRow`
- **I25** (M12) Steam released into open air hardly condenses: a cell is one unit of matter, so a steam cell holds the 2,257 MJ/m³ of the water it came from, and air at its physical 0.0012 MJ/m³·K needs about 23,500 cells of itself warmed from 20 to 100 °C to take it (64 steam cells in a 128×192 box cooled to a mean of 96 °C in 15 s and none condensed); steam condenses only against cold solids or as the air is replaced. Real steam condenses within a second because it mixes with a far larger volume of air than its own cell. A mixing exchange for gases (a larger coefficient and capacity share for a gas cell) or an expansion of steam into several cells would restore it. `src/air.ts`, `src/shaders/air.wgsl.ts` `airExchangeWGSL`
- **I26** (M12) Hot things lose heat only to the air, never by radiation: a dull surface radiates about as much as still air carries away at 150 °C, 3 times as much at 500 °C and 12 times at 1,000 °C, so lava and molten metal stay hot several times too long (the 16×16 metal block of `tests/air-heat.test.ts` is at 944 °C 125 s after 1,000 °C, where an oxidized steel bar would be near 600 °C at the METAL row's constant heat capacity and near 740 °C with steel's real one, I30: `redHotBar`). A loss per exposed cell by its emissivity fits the cell–air exchange at negligible cost; warming what the radiation reaches waits for the light field (M14). `coolingBlock`'s target is still air's convection alone, so with radiation it is measured at emissivity 0. `src/shaders/air.wgsl.ts` `airExchangeWGSL`, `docs/research/physical-properties.md` (Radiated heat)
- **I27** (M12) Liquids level about 5 to 10 times slower than real: a column of water 100 cells high (14 cm) takes about 3 s at 1x to drop to a flat pool, real water well under a second, so a pour piles up at 45° while it runs. A liquid cell moves at most one cell per dispatch and only into an empty neighbor of its own row. Faster leveling (for example a whole row of surface cells shifting toward a lower neighbor per dispatch) only if it fits the frame budget (`npm run bench`). `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `spreadRow`
- **I28** (M8) Water on lava boils only by conduction at the contact, so a layer 1 to 2 cm deep takes tens of seconds to minutes to boil away. Real water also sinks into cracks, mixes and is stirred by its own steam bubbles, which boils it much faster. A mixing between a liquid and a much hotter neighbor would model that. `src/shaders/heat.wgsl.ts` `exchangeHeat`
- **I12** Choose a license for CorniSand. With no license file nobody may reuse the code; a permissive license (MIT, Apache-2.0) lets others reuse it without requiring them, or this project, to publish source. Decide before accepting outside contributions.
- **I29** (M12) Convection has no onset: two cells of one liquid swap with a chance for any density difference, so a layer heated gently from below stirs at once, where real water conducts until its Rayleigh number passes about 1,708. A water layer 3 cells deep across 1 °C (Rayleigh 970) carries 2.85 times the heat conduction would, one 8 cells deep across 2 °C (36,780) 7.3 times, where real water carries 1 and about 3.3 times (`heatedLayer`). `src/physics.ts` `convectionChance`, `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `convect`
- **I30** (M12) A row's heat capacity is one number: steel's runs from 440 J/kg·K at 20 °C through a peak near 735 °C to 650 above 900 °C (EN 1993-1-2), which moves a red-hot bar cooling in air by more than 100 °C (`redHotBar`, full). `src/elements.ts`, `src/shaders/heat.wgsl.ts`
- **I34** (M12) Powders fall with the default drag whatever their grain size: sand settles at 1.3 m/s instead of about 2 and lands from 1 m (734 cells) after 0.93 s instead of 0.64 (`freeFall`); a grain size per row would set the drag (`docs/research/physical-properties.md`, Grain size). `src/elements.ts`
- **I35** (M12) A liquid spreads at the speed of a layer `REF_DEPTH` deep whatever its own depth, so a thick liquid's front does not slow as the fifth root of time: in `viscousSpread` (lava, a column 14 wide and 40 high) the front is 29 cells from the wall after 3 s (28 to 30 on seeds 1 to 3), real lava 65, and 76 to 130 after 30 s (real 103), a ratio of 3.5 (the mean of three seeds) against the real 1.58. `src/shaders/ca.wgsl.ts` `viscousGo`
- **I38** One rock, basalt (STONE), stands for every rock. Others behave visibly differently: granite melts into a lava far thicker than basalt's (with I39), sandstone is about a fifth lighter and breaks under a weaker blast, and marble and limestone never melt: at about 800 to 900 °C they break down into quicklime powder and carbon dioxide, a reaction (M8.4). `src/elements.ts`
- **I39** Lava flows and holds heat the same whatever it melted from, where real melts differ widely: basalt's lava is 10 to 100 Pa·s, a granite-like melt a thousand to a million times thicker or more. - **I36** A game-time clock beside the speed readout: the time elapsed since the scene loaded in simulated time (supersteps run ÷ 240 per second, formatted like `7:00.0`), not the wall clock, so it stays true at any speed, when frames drop, and at 1/4x to 8x (a 7-minute melt is seen after 52 s at 8x, the clock still reading 7:00). It counts supersteps in `main.ts` (reset by a scene load, frozen by a pause, I37) and needs no GPU read-back; it is only "real time" if B19 is fixed or the display is 60 Hz, since the physical scale is 4 supersteps per frame at 60 frames per second at 1x. - **I37** Pause and rewind. Pause is a speed of 0 (the frame still runs the brush and the render, no superstep). Rewind cannot run the simulation backwards (the automaton loses information), so it restores a past state: snapshots of the GPU buffers cost about 69 MB each at 2560×1340 plus the air and flyers (I32), so a ring of them is affordable only for a few seconds or at a smaller scale. The cheaper way, inferred from the bit-identical reruns, is a keyframe every few seconds plus a log of the input (brush, tool and speed changes, with the superstep they happened at): rewinding to time t restores the last keyframe before t and replays the log up to t, faster than real time (the GPU runs 8x at 32 supersteps per frame). It needs the replay to be exactly deterministic, air, flyers and the brush included, and a new input after a rewind drops the future. Decide the rewind span and whether stepping forward one superstep or frame while paused comes with it. `src/simulation.ts`, `src/cell-field-buffers.ts` `read`, `src/main.ts`
- **I40** The heat capacity of a powder has two conventions: SAND and GUNPOWDER hold their bulk density's worth (1.3 and 1.4 MJ/m³·K), while the crushed forms (gravel, snow, broken glass) keep their parent solid's, since a cell is one unit of matter and a crush keeps the temperature. A cell of sand therefore takes about half the heat a cell of gravel does for the same rise. Decide one convention for powders, with a scenario (a poured pile heated from below). `src/elements.ts`
