# Roadmap

Direction: elements that behave by real physics, with The Powder Toy as the inspiration for what to simulate, in the browser on the GPU, on a larger playing field, then what only a GPU can afford. The reasoning and the frame budget are in `docs/powder-toy.md`. Every milestone keeps the frame budget set there on the reference RTX 4080, measured by `npm run bench`, with every size and resolution a setting so M10 can scale it down (`docs/decisions.md`). IDs are stable. Next ID: M17.

Order: M8.1, M12.13, M12.1, M12.12, M12.2 to M12.4, M15.1, M8.2, M13, M14, M12.6, M12.7, M8.3, M8.4, M15.2, M8.5 to M8.7, M12.8 to M12.11, M7, M9, M16, M10. To be confirmed, outside the order until then: M12.5.

Why: the approved plan first; then a scenario for every physical property and mechanic, so the gaps are known before the physics slices; then the cheap physics the scenarios already measure, on M8.1's new materials, and a readout that shows those scenarios' numbers in the game; the smooth look and the light once fire and the new materials exist to draw; depth and moisture before chemistry, which reuses their per-cell amounts; the light before M7, whose optics build on it. A finished item leaves this line in the commit that finishes it.

## M8 Element catalogue

The Powder Toy's elements that need only what the game simulates before M7 (heat, fire, air and pressure), slice by slice, with real physical properties. Each models a real material where one exists. Done when every slice is done: each element has a scene test of its main behavior, the real-world scenarios its slices name pass, and the frame budget holds.

- **M8.1** Materials and the element picker (`docs/plans/m8.1-materials.md`).
- **M8.2** Fire and explosives: slow burning (wood, coal, fuse, ignition cord), acrylic (it burns; with M8.1's aluminium it makes the `iceOnMetal` scenario), dust that burns in a flash, flammable gas, plasma, cold flame, pressure-set explosives (C-4, nitroglycerin, diesel), TNT that goes off as a whole (backlog I18), bombs, thermite, fireworks, a cold-set explosive, and stored energy (uranium heats under pressure, vibranium, the singularity). Properties: ignition temperature, heat of combustion and burn speed, oxygen in the air, a flash point apart from the ignition temperature (a lit match goes out in diesel and lights petrol: `matchInDiesel`), a fuel that smoulders before it catches (an ignition delay that shortens as it heats), a dust's burn speed from its grain size (M12.1: flour dispersed in air explodes, a heap smoulders: `flourDust`), and soot where oxygen runs short (I16). Drawn with an explosion's flash, smoke as dark as what burned, fireworks as colored glowing flyers, cold flame's blue and thermite's glare.
- **M8.3** Air, gases and force: heavy gases (carbon dioxide that pours and pools: `heavyGas`, dry ice), gases that expand when heated or cool when compressed (Boyle gas, refrigerant), vents and vacuum, accelerators and decelerators, repellers, anti-air dust, glow that lights up under pressure, goo, cornstarch in water (solid under a blow, liquid under slow stress), concrete, dust colored by its speed, a wall that opens under pressure. Properties: boiling points that follow the pressure (a pressure cooker boils water at 120 °C, a vacuum boils it at room temperature: `pressureCooker`), which needs the air's pressure on a physical scale (it has no unit today, `src/air.ts`).
- **M8.4** Chemistry: reactions between elements in contact: salt dissolves into salt water up to its solubility, clay and water make paste that bakes into brick, acid dissolves and base neutralizes it, hydrogen burns with oxygen into water (oxygen and liquid oxygen), lithium and rubidium explode in water, iron rusts into broken metal, platinum speeds reactions, soda water fizzes, virus spreads and soap cures it, antimatter annihilates, sponge and gel soak up water (M12.7's moisture), yeast grows, freeze powder. Properties: a dissolved amount per cell, so salt lowers water's freezing point (salt on ice makes brine at −21 °C: `saltOnIce`), dissolving heats or cools (an instant cold pack), and a gas dissolves less as it warms (warm soda goes flat faster).
- **M8.5** Life: Life-like and Generations rules (Conway's Life and its variants, TPT's set of about 24, rules from LifeWiki's public catalogue) on painted cells, in a pass of their own outside the Margolus CA.
- **M8.6** Special and growing: clone, breakable clone, converter, void, warp, TRON, plants that drink water and grow, vines, seeds that grow trees, bizarre (it melts when cold).
- **M8.7** Portals and pipes: portals that carry what enters one to another of the same channel, drawn with a swirl around them; pipes that carry particles along their length.

## M12 Physics realism

What the real-world scenarios (`AGENTS.md`, "Real-world scenarios") measure as missing, and the properties that show at the grid's scale (`docs/research/physical-properties.md`, `docs/research/volume-changes.md`), one slice each. Each slice's plan writes its scenarios before its mechanics. M12.3, M12.7, M8.4's dissolved amount and, if confirmed, M12.5 each change how much heat a cell holds: M12.3's plan designs that bookkeeping so the others extend it. Several slices keep state in a cell's spare bits (M12.6, M12.7, M12.9, M12.11, M8.4, M15.2, and M12.5 if confirmed): the first of them to be planned divides the bits among all. Done when every slice is done (M12.5 only if confirmed): the scenarios that wait on it, and those its plan writes, pass, and the frame budget holds.

- **M12.13** Scenario coverage: every physical property of the element table and every physical mechanic of `docs/simulation.md` (heat, phase changes, movement, the air, fire) gets a real-world scenario, built and passing, built and waiting on a named item (a new backlog line where none exists), or named out of reach with its reason. A test lists each property and mechanic with its scenarios and fails when one has none, so a later property cannot land without one (`AGENTS.md`, "Real-world scenarios"). It runs after M8.1, whose scenarios it audits too, and before the other slices, so their gaps are known first.
- **M12.1** Grain size (backlog I34): each powder falls and drifts by its grain size from data instead of one tuned drag: sand lands from 1 m at its real time (`freeFall`), gravel ignores the wind, flour (M8.2) hangs in the air.
- **M12.12** Granular flow (B17, B18), after M12.1 changes how grains fall: sand runs through an outlet at the rate real grains do as it widens, and a pile poured by a stream keeps sand's angle of repose at any size (`hourglass`).
- **M12.2** Radiated heat (I26, with B16): hot things lose heat by radiation as well as to the air, so a red-hot bar dulls and a lava pool skins over at their real pace (`redHotBar` fast, `lavaCrust`). Warming what the radiation reaches is M14's.
- **M12.3** Heat capacity by temperature (I30): steel's capacity follows its temperature, so a bar out of the forge lingers near 740 °C (`redHotBar` full).
- **M12.4** Film boiling: a liquid on a surface far above its boiling point floats on its own vapor: water skates on a hot pan and outlasts a drop on a cooler one, liquid nitrogen skitters (`leidenfrost`, `nitrogenOnWater`).
- **M12.5** Volume changes (I22), to be confirmed: matter keeps its amount while its volume changes: water grows 9% as it freezes, steam may fill several cells (I25), and, at more cost per cell, a mercury thermometer reads. Confirmed or dropped once M8.1 brings mercury and glass, on the `thermometer` scene; nothing else waits on it (`docs/research/volume-changes.md`).
- **M12.6** Liquids by depth and convection (I35, I29, I27, B15): a thick liquid's front slows as it thins, a gently heated layer conducts before it stirs, a water column levels at its real speed, a cup stirred by its own cold meltwater mixes within a minute (`viscousSpread`, `heatedLayer`, `damBreak`, `iceCup`).
- **M12.7** Moisture and surface tension: sand soaks up water, darkens and stands in steep damp walls; water drains through gravel far faster than through sand, beads into drops, climbs narrow gaps and stops spreading on wax (`sandCastle`, `drainage`, `capillary`, `puddleOnWax`).
- **M12.8** Erosion: flowing water picks up sand and drops it where it slows: channels, deltas, a sand dam that fails when overtopped.
- **M12.9** Strength and support: pieces cut loose fall instead of floating; damp sand, snow, an ice shelf and loaded glass sag and break.
- **M12.10** Restitution: a steel ball bounces, a grain landing on loose sand kicks up its neighbors, a stone thrown into water splashes.
- **M12.11** Supercooling: still water stays liquid below 0 °C until something seeds it, then turns to slush at once; freezing rain glazes what it lands on.

## M15 Readout and views

What the cells hold, shown without the tests (`docs/research/physical-properties.md`, "Observables"). Done when each readout line has a unit test (`tests/readout.test.ts`), each view a display test, and the heat budget agrees with the tests' total heat on a sealed box.

- **M15.1** The pointer readout gives a phase change's progress ("Ice · 0 °C · melting 41%") and real units (speed in m/s, the depth and pressure under a liquid, the density at the cell's temperature); a scenario opened from the Scenes menu shows its measurement beside its target (I33); views of dye (a brush that marks matter without changing it, to watch convection and mixing), speed, heat flow and crack risk.
- **M15.2** What reads what a cell holds beyond its element (water in sand, a dissolved amount) or its spare bits, after M12.7 and M8.4 add them: its mass and heat in joules, a panel with the world's total heat, a cell's peak temperature (with temper colors on heated steel), its age and origin ("set from lava 12 s ago"), the distance it travelled.

## M13 Smooth look

Liquids drawn as smooth surfaces with highlights, fire as soft flames, powders shaded as heaps, solids bevelled and textured in world space (brick courses, wood grain, brushed metal), round shapes where a cell spans several pixels and sharp edges on high-density screens; the newer materials drawn as themselves (glass see-through with a bright edge, metals with a sheen, sparkling snow, gravel in several tones, mercury as a mirror-bright liquid); thrown grains as short streaks; embers above fire and ripples on still water if they earn their cost (`docs/research/rendering.md`, "The smooth look"). The cells look stays as it is. Done when each effect has a deterministic display test against the same scene without its cause, and the renderer stays within its 1 ms budget (`docs/rendering.md`, "Budget").

## M14 Light

Fire, lava and hot metal light their surroundings, walls and heaps cast soft shadows, and a setting runs from daylight to darkness, where only hot things light the scene. The same light field carries radiated heat to what it reaches, so ice melts beside lava it does not touch (`docs/research/rendering.md`, "Lighting"). M7 adds each element's optics to it. Done when scene tests show the light on the two sides of an obstacle differing by its shadow, a real-world scenario of ice melting beside lava meets its target from the radiation law, and the light field costs at most 1 ms per 1x frame.

## M7 Electricity and energy particles

Sparks travel through conductors and power elements (heaters, switches, batteries), and photons, neutrons and electrons share cells with matter. It also brings the elements that need sparks or energy particles (TPT's electronics, powered materials and sensors, beams, pistons and frames, the radioactive elements that emit particles with their half-lives, quartz, filter, distilled water, noble gas, fog and rime, resist, shield, lightning and thunder), and the electrical and optical properties of every element: resistivity, the voltage a junction of two metals makes from heat (a thermocouple: `thermocouple`), transparency, reflection, colored light through tinted matter, refraction that splits white light in a prism (`prism`) and how much sunlight a surface absorbs, carried by M14's light field. Drawn as sparks running along wires, photons and beams as glowing lines, lightning with a flash. Done when scene tests show a spark crossing a wire at a known speed, a powered heater warming water, and light reflected or absorbed by element, and the `thermocouple` and `prism` scenarios pass.

## M9 Newtonian gravity

A gravity field computed from the mass layout pulls particles toward each other. And the elements that need it: black and white holes (drawn with the light bent around them), the gravity bomb, gravitons, the gravity pump. Done when scene tests show a cloud of particles clumping and a light particle orbiting a heavy body.

## M16 Magnetism

Magnets pull iron, nickel and cobalt, iron filings line up along the field, and a magnetic material loses its pull above its Curie temperature (iron at 770 °C). The field is solved on a coarse grid as M9's gravity is (`docs/research/physical-properties.md`, "Magnetism"); electromagnets also wait for currents with a strength, a voltage solve beyond M7's sparks. Done when scene tests show filings lining up along a magnet's field and iron above 770 °C no longer pulled.

## M10 Scaling down

The settings shrink to a default that is still as fun (no field, grid or step count bigger than it needs to be), then the game is measured on weaker hardware (real, or the dev machine's integrated GPU) and either trimmed further where a large speed gain costs little, or given presets or an in-app benchmark that picks settings, the drawing included (each effect on or off, the field and light resolutions; the smooth look hides a smaller grid's larger cells). Done when the default holds 60 fps on the RTX 4080 with headroom, and a named low-end setup (at least the integrated GPU) holds 60 fps with its preset or detected settings.
